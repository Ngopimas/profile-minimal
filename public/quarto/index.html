One sheet · two folds · eight pages

Quarto

How a printed page is physically made, printed on the page it describes.

This essay is printed on a single sheet. Scroll, and the sheet is folded, sewn and trimmed into the booklet that explains it. Nothing here is a picture of text. The paper is a mesh on your graphics card, and the ink is this very paragraph: a live piece of the document that you can select, copy and tab through, even where it is printed upside down.

In this browser the sheet stays flat and unfolded, so the eight pages simply follow one another.

A Quarto · sig. A · outer forme · K C M Y

I Fold

A press does not print pages. It prints sheets, several pages at a time. Fold a sheet once and you have a folio of four pages; twice, a quarto of eight; three times, an octavo of sixteen. The names count leaves, not folds: a quarto is a sheet in four.

For the pages to fall in order after folding, they must be laid out of order before it. This is imposition. Here pages 1, 4, 5 and 8 share one side of the sheet, the outer forme, and 2, 3, 6 and 7 the other. Pages 4 and 5 stand head to head with 1 and 8, which is why half the sheet read upside down when you arrived.

Nothing on this sheet was arranged by hand. The program folds the mesh first, counts the layers from front to back, and only then decides which page is printed on which panel, and which way up.

II Ink

Ink is either on the paper or it is not. A press cannot print a paler black, so every grey and every photograph is made of dots: large for dark tones, small for light ones, on a grid too fine to notice. This is the halftone.

Colour takes four inks, cyan, magenta, yellow and black, each on its own plate with its own grid. The grids are turned to different angles, commonly black at 45°, cyan at 15°, magenta at 75° and yellow at 0°, so the dots gather into small rosettes instead of a moiré.

III Press

Most printing today is offset lithography. The plate is flat. Its image areas hold greasy ink, its blank areas hold a film of water, and the two refuse to mix. The plate never touches the paper: it prints onto a rubber blanket, and the blanket prints onto the sheet. That detour is the offset.

Each ink has its own printing unit, and the sheet runs through all four in about a second, commonly black first, then cyan, magenta and yellow. The four impressions must land on one another to within about a tenth of a millimetre. That is register, and the first sheets of every run are spent finding it.

4 Quarto · outer forme

IV Thread

A folded sheet is called a signature, after the letter the printer set at the foot of its first page so that the binder could gather the sections in order. This one is signed A, at the foot of page 1.

A thick book is many signatures sewn to one another through their folds. A single signature needs three holes and one length of thread. The needle goes out through the middle of the fold, comes back in at the head, runs down the whole gutter, goes out at the tail and returns through the middle, where the two ends are knotted across the long stitch.

You are at the centre of the booklet, the one opening where the thread shows. Wire staples do the same job faster, and the trade calls that saddle stitching. A glued paperback has its folds milled away, and nothing but adhesive holds its leaves.

5 Quarto · outer forme · centre

V Knife

Until now the booklet was unfinished. The fold across the head still joined its leaves in pairs. Such closed folds are called bolts, and you tore two of them to get here: this opening and the first one were sealed. Books were once sold like this, and readers opened them with a paper knife as they went.

A bindery trims three edges: the fore-edge, then the head and the tail. The spine is never cut. The knives take off the bolts, the ragged edges and a few millimetres of margin, so anything meant to run off the page is printed about three millimetres past the trim line. That overhang is the bleed. The hairlines in the corners are crop marks. They show the knife where to fall, and they leave with the waste.

Scroll, and the knives come down: the front knife first, then the two side knives together.

6 Quarto · inner forme

VI Screen

In July 2026 this page could not have shipped. Text inside a 3D scene was a picture of text, or an invisible copy floated on top, unless you held a trial token. Since Chrome 157 any canvas can adopt real elements through the HTML-in-Canvas API. The browser lays them out, paints them and hands each one to the GPU as a texture, here through the API’s WebGL 2 entry point. The page reports back where every element ended up, so a click still lands on the right word after two folds and a turn.

Try it. Select this sentence. Press Tab and watch the focus ring travel across the paper.

Each of the eight pages is one section of an ordinary article. Where the API is missing they stack up as plain text, and nothing is lost but the paper. Chrome’s documentation has the details.
